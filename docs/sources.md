# Ratio sources

Researched 2026-11-13. Not shown on the page. Every ratio in `rice.js` is explained here; change both together.

## What the ratio means

`ratio` = grams of **total water** per gram of dry rice, for rinsed rice in a rice cooker. Total water includes the water clinging to the rice after rinsing, because the page's target weight is read off a scale with the wet rice already in the pot.

## Conversions used

**Clinging water: +0.10.** Most recipes give the water added *after* rinsing and draining. Japan's Ministry of Agriculture (MAFF) states that milled rice gains about 10% of its weight in clinging water during washing, and that water amounts by weight include it. So where a source gives water added to rinsed, drained rice, 0.10 is added to get total water.
Limit: the 10% figure was measured on Japanese short-grain rice. It is applied to jasmine and basmati here with no source confirming it for long-grain. If long-grain results are too wet, this is the first assumption to revisit.

**Volume to weight.** Water: 1 ml = 1 g. US cup = 240 ml. Dry long-grain rice per US cup: 185 g (USDA FoodData Central, FDC 168877, "Rice, white, long-grain, regular, raw": 1 cup = 185 g). One recipe source weighs its own cup of jasmine at 205 g (Hungry Huy); both are used to give a range. The cup size cancels out, so the same factors apply to a 180 ml rice-cooker cup.
- 1 cup water per cup rice = 1.17 to 1.30 g/g
- 1¼ cups per cup = 1.46 to 1.62 g/g
- 1½ cups per cup = 1.76 to 1.95 g/g

## Values

### Jasmine
Total-water range across sources: 1.10 to 1.72.
- S3: 1.00 added + 0.10 = **1.10**
- S6: 240 / 205 = 1.17 added + 0.10 = **1.27**
- S5: 1.2 cups = 1.40 to 1.56 added + 0.10 = **1.50 to 1.66**
- S2: 1¼ cups = 1.46 to 1.62 added + 0.10 = **1.56 to 1.72**

The two sources that weigh (S3, S6) sit low; the two that measure by cup (S2, S5) sit high.

- **grainy = 1.25.** Top of the weighed sources (S6: 1.27). Not lower, because S3's 1.10 is the single lowest figure found.
- **fluffy = 1.40.** Middle of the full range (1.41). Below the cup-based sources because they conflict with the weighed ones.

Confidence: moderate.

### Basmati
No rice-cooker source gives basmati by weight. Total-water range: 1.56 to 2.10.
- 1¼ cups per cup (the common "rinsed basmati" figure in recipe sources; no manufacturer source found for it): 1.46 to 1.62 added + 0.10 = **1.56 to 1.72**
- S2: 1½ cups = 1.76 to 1.95 added + 0.10 = **1.86 to 2.05**
- S4 (covered pan, more evaporation than a rice cooker): 2.00 added + 0.10 = **2.10**. Upper bound only.
- S10: 1.5 to 2 cups, consistent with the above.

- **grainy = 1.65.** Middle of the 1¼-cup range. Weakest support of the long-grain values: the 1¼ figure comes from recipe sites, not a manufacturer.
- **fluffy = 1.90.** Lower part of the Zojirushi range, kept below the covered-pan figure. Flagged as the first value to test.

Confidence: lowest of the three types. Depends on the cup-to-gram conversion (about ±0.15) and on the clinging-water assumption.

### Sushi (Japanese short-grain)
- **onigiri = 1.35.** Plain everyday rice. Middle of S1's rice-cooker range (1.3 to 1.4, clinging water included). Cross-check: S8 and S9 give 200 ml per 150 g = 1.33.
- **rolling = 1.25.** Vinegared rice is cooked with less water. S7: 1.20 added + 0.10 = 1.30. S9: 190 / 150 = 1.27 (1.37 if clinging water is not included). Chosen 1.25, about 7% below onigiri, so the difference between the two options is noticeable; sources describe the reduction as 5 to 10%.
- **poke = 1.30.** **No source gives a poke-specific water amount.** Poke is plain rice (S11), and this option is meant to be less sticky than onigiri, so it takes the bottom of S1's range. S1 supports adjusting within the range for firmness. Stickiness depends mostly on the rice variety and rinsing; water changes it only a little.

Confidence: likely right for onigiri and rolling (government source working by weight); poke is a judgement call inside a sourced range.

## Not supported by any source
- A separate number for poke rice (see above).
- Small-batch compensation: no source gives an amount of extra water for small batches. S2 sets a minimum of 1 rice-cooker cup (about 140 to 150 g) for jasmine and basmati.
- The 10% clinging-water figure for long-grain rice.
