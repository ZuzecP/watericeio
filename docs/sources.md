# Ratio sources

Researched 2026-11-13 (rice cooker, then hob, oven and the generic rice types). Not shown on the page. Every ratio in `rice.js` is explained here; change both together.

## What the ratio means

`ratio` = grams of **total water** per gram of dry rice, for rinsed rice. Total water includes the water clinging to the rice after rinsing, because the page's target weight is read off a scale with the wet rice already in the pot.

## Conversions used

**Clinging water: +0.10.** Most recipes give the water added *after* rinsing and draining. Japan's Ministry of Agriculture (MAFF) states that milled rice gains about 10% of its weight in clinging water during washing, and that water amounts by weight include it. So where a source gives water added to rinsed, drained rice, 0.10 is added to get total water.
Limit: the 10% figure was measured on Japanese short-grain rice. It is applied to jasmine and basmati here with no source confirming it for long-grain. If long-grain results are too wet, this is the first assumption to revisit.

**Volume to weight.** Water: 1 ml = 1 g. US cup = 240 ml. Dry long-grain rice per US cup: 185 g (USDA FoodData Central, FDC 168877, "Rice, white, long-grain, regular, raw": 1 cup = 185 g). One recipe source weighs its own cup of jasmine at 205 g (Hungry Huy); both are used to give a range. The cup size cancels out, so the same factors apply to a 180 ml rice-cooker cup.
- 1 cup water per cup rice = 1.17 to 1.30 g/g
- 1¼ cups per cup = 1.46 to 1.62 g/g
- 1½ cups per cup = 1.76 to 1.95 g/g

Dry short-grain rice per US cup: 200 g (USDA FDC 168931). Medium-grain: 195 g (USDA FDC 168879).

**Unrinsed amounts are already total water.** Where a source gives water for unrinsed rice (S12 to S16), that amount is the total and nothing is added. S12 confirms the size of the clinging water independently of S1: it tells cooks who rinse to remove 2 tablespoons of water per cup of rice, about 16% of the rice weight.

## Values

### Jasmine
Total-water range across sources: 1.10 to 1.72.
- S3: 1.00 added + 0.10 = **1.10**
- S6: 240 / 205 = 1.17 added + 0.10 = **1.27**
- S5: 1.2 cups = 1.40 to 1.56 added + 0.10 = **1.50 to 1.66**
- S2: 1¼ cups = 1.46 to 1.62 added + 0.10 = **1.56 to 1.72**

The two sources that weigh (S3, S6) sit low; the two that measure by cup (S2, S5) sit high.

- **grainy = 1.25.** Top of the weighed sources (S6: 1.27). Not lower, because S3's 1.10 is the single lowest figure found.
- **fluffy = 1.40.** Middle of the full range (1.41). Below the cup-based sources because they conflict with the weighed ones.

Confidence: moderate.

### Basmati
No rice-cooker source gives basmati by weight. Total-water range: 1.56 to 2.10.
- 1¼ cups per cup (the common "rinsed basmati" figure in recipe sources; no manufacturer source found for it): 1.46 to 1.62 added + 0.10 = **1.56 to 1.72**
- S2: 1½ cups = 1.76 to 1.95 added + 0.10 = **1.86 to 2.05**
- S4 (covered pan, more evaporation than a rice cooker): 2.00 added + 0.10 = **2.10**. Upper bound only.
- S10: 1.5 to 2 cups, consistent with the above.

- **grainy = 1.65.** Middle of the 1¼-cup range. Weakest support of the long-grain values: the 1¼ figure comes from recipe sites, not a manufacturer.
- **fluffy = 1.90.** Lower part of the Zojirushi range, kept below the covered-pan figure. Flagged as the first value to test.

Confidence: lowest of the three types. Depends on the cup-to-gram conversion (about ±0.15) and on the clinging-water assumption.

### Sushi (Japanese short-grain)
- **onigiri = 1.35.** Plain everyday rice. Middle of S1's rice-cooker range (1.3 to 1.4, clinging water included). Cross-check: S8 and S9 give 200 ml per 150 g = 1.33.
- **rolling = 1.25.** Vinegared rice is cooked with less water. S7: 1.20 added + 0.10 = 1.30. S9: 190 / 150 = 1.27 (1.37 if clinging water is not included). Chosen 1.25, about 7% below onigiri, so the difference between the two options is noticeable; sources describe the reduction as 5 to 10%.
- **poke = 1.30.** **No source gives a poke-specific water amount.** Poke is plain rice (S11), and this option is meant to be less sticky than onigiri, so it takes the bottom of S1's range. S1 supports adjusting within the range for firmness. Stickiness depends mostly on the rice variety and rinsing; water changes it only a little.

Confidence: likely right for onigiri and rolling (government source working by weight); poke is a judgement call inside a sourced range.

Sources disagree with each other more than rice types differ within one source (jasmine alone runs from 1.2 to 2 cups), so types were compared within each source and by weight.

- **Short grain** (Japanese, sushi) is lowest, and its sources agree closely.
- **Jasmine** takes about 15 to 20% less water than other long grain in the sources that treat it separately (S5, S13, S16).
- **Basmati and generic long grain** take the same amount in S12/S14 and S15. On the page, `long-grain` uses the basmati values.
- **Medium grain is not offered.** By weight it sits near long grain in S12 and S15 (about 1.85), while medium-grain Calrose is commonly cooked like Japanese short grain. No producer source was checked, so it is left out.
- **Brown and parboiled** need roughly 15 to 40% more water and longer cooking. Not researched to a usable value; not offered.
- Wild, risotto and glutinous rice use other techniques and do not fit this calculator.

## Hob and oven

Assumed conditions: a heavy pot or dish, fully covered so that only a little steam escapes, lid kept on. For the oven, the water goes in boiling.

**Oven = hob.** S12 and S15 both use the same water for the stovetop and the oven under those conditions. One table serves both methods. No source covers Japanese short grain in the oven; the same rule is applied there without a source.

**Hob against rice cooker.** S1 gives 1.5 for a pot against 1.3 to 1.4 for a rice cooker, about 0.15 more. Western sources use the same cup amounts for both. The hob values below sit 0.10 to 0.25 above the rice cooker values.

### Short grain, hob and oven
- S1: **1.50** (the standard for pot cooking, clinging water included).
- S8: 300 g rice to 400 ml, rinsed and drained: 1.33 + 0.10 = **1.43**.
- S15: 1¼ cups per cup, unrinsed: 300 / 200 = **1.50**.

- **sushi onigiri = 1.50**, **rolling = 1.40** (about 7% less, as for the rice cooker), **poke = 1.45** (between the two; no poke-specific source).

Confidence: likely right.

### Plain short grain (`short-grain`), all methods
The same rice as sushi, cooked plain, with a soft and firm pair in place of the sushi styles.
- Rice cooker: **firm = 1.30**, **soft = 1.40**, the two ends of S1's rice cooker range.
- Hob and oven: **firm = 1.45**, **soft = 1.55**, the same 0.05 either side of S1's 1.50. Soft at 1.55 is slightly above every source figure; it is a judgement call.

### Jasmine, hob and oven
Total-water range: 1.56 to 2.6.
- S13: 1¼ cups, unrinsed: 300 / 185 = **1.62**
- S5: 1.2 cups: **1.56 to 1.66** (as above)
- S3, covered pan method on the same page: 120 g rice to 240 ml water, rinsed: 2.00 + 0.10 = **2.10**
- S16: 1¾ cups: **2.27**. S15: 2 cups: **2.59** (US-grown jasmine, treated like any long grain).

- **grainy = 1.45**, **fluffy = 1.65**. Kept near the sources that treat jasmine separately (S5, S13); 1.65 matches them, 1.45 is 0.20 below, mirroring the rice cooker pair.

Confidence: the weakest of the hob values. Grainy at 1.45 is below every source figure.

### Basmati and long grain, hob and oven
Total-water range: 1.95 to 2.6.
- S12, S14, S16 (basmati): 1½ cups, unrinsed: 360 / 185 = **1.95**
- S4: 2 : 1 by weight, rinsed: **2.10**
- S15, S16 (long grain): 2 cups: **2.59**

- **fluffy = 2.00**, between S12/S14 and S4. **grainy = 1.80**, 0.20 below, for separate grains; below the source figures, as a firmer option.

Confidence: moderate.

## Not supported by any source
- A separate number for poke rice (see above).
- Small-batch compensation: no source gives an amount of extra water for small batches. S2 sets a minimum of 1 rice-cooker cup (about 140 to 150 g) for jasmine and basmati.
- The 10% clinging-water figure for long-grain rice (S12's 2 tablespoons per cup points the same way, at about 16%).
- Japanese short grain in the oven.
- The firmer hob values (jasmine grainy 1.45, long grain grainy 1.80) and short grain soft 1.55 sit just outside the published figures.
